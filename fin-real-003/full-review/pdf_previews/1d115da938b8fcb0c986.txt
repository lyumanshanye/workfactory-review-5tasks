0.1 1 Purpose and Scope
This manual states the finance and accounting policies of Tannahill Mechanical Contractors, Inc. and the
procedures the finance department follows to carry them out. It applies to every employee who prepares,
reviews, approves, or files financial and tax information for the company, and to every branch, shop, and
field location the company operates.
The company is a commercial HVAC and plumbing contractor working out of two branches, with an
in-house fleet of service trucks and vans and a parts warehouse. Revenue comes from service contracts,
construction projects, and emergency call-out work. The finance department keeps the general ledger,
prepares the monthly close, manages vendor and subcontractor payments, administers payroll, and
prepares the federal and state tax returns before they go to the outside CPA firm for review and e-filing.

0.1.1 1.1 Objectives
The policies in this manual exist to keep the company's books and filings reliable, to make the work of
one accountant checkable by another, and to give the outside auditors and the CPA firm a consistent
basis for their work. Where a policy and a written direction from the Controller conflict, the Controller's
direction governs for that matter and is documented in the file. Where this manual is silent, staff follow
the professional practice of a careful accountant in the construction contracting industry.

0.1.4 1.4 Accounting basis
The company keeps its books on the accrual basis in accordance with accounting principles generally
accepted in the United States. The statements are prepared on a calendar-year basis. Tax computations
follow the Internal Revenue Code and the regulations and forms prescribed for the year of the return;
where a book treatment and a tax treatment differ, the workpapers carry both and the difference is
scheduled.

0.1.5 1.5 Changes to this manual
The Controller approves every change to this manual before it takes effect. Each approved change is
recorded in the revision history with its effective date and a short statement of what changed.
Superseded revisions are kept in the policy file for seven years so that a position taken in an earlier year
can be read against the policy that was in effect at the time.

0.2 2 Documentation Standards
Every schedule, workpaper, and supporting file the finance department produces follows the standards
in this section. The standards exist so that a reviewer who did not prepare the work can follow it without
a walk-through.

0.2.1 2.1 File naming and organization
Workpapers are organized by tax year and by schedule type. File names state the period and the
schedule: for example, the fixed asset detail for the 2025 return is kept in the 2025 depreciation and
amortization workpapers. Source documents — invoices, titles, agreements, closing statements — are
filed by vendor and date in the document management system, and the workpapers cite them by
document number.

0.2.2 2.2 Supporting documentation
Every number on a schedule carries support. A cost in the fixed asset register is supported by the vendor
invoice and the approval that authorized the purchase. An intangible in the amortization schedule is
supported by the agreement that created it and the closing statement that priced it. A vehicle in the fleet
register is supported by the title, the registration, and the purchase file. Support is filed when the entry is
made, not later.

0.2.3 2.3 Review evidence
Work that a second person reviews carries that person's initials and the date of review. A review is
evidenced on the schedule itself or on a checklist that lists the steps performed. Initialing a schedule
means the reviewer has performed the steps the checklist names for that schedule and found the work as
presented, not that the reviewer has glanced at it.

0.2.4 2.4 Corrections
An error found in a filed or signed schedule is corrected in the period it is found, with a note stating what
was corrected and why. Correcting entries are supported like any other entry. A correction that changes
a figure already given to a lender, surety, or tax authority is reported to the Controller the day it is found.

0.3 3 Fixed Asset and Depreciation Policy
The fixed asset function exists to answer three questions with support a reviewer can follow: what the
company owns, what it cost, and how much of the cost has been recovered. Every procedure in this
section serves one of those questions.

0.3.1 3.1 Capitalization and Classification
The company capitalizes an item of property or equipment when it has a useful life of more than one
year and a cost of $2,500 or more. Items below the threshold are expensed when purchased. Cost
includes freight, sales tax, installation, and the other costs of putting the asset into service.
Capitalized items are classified in the fixed asset register by type and by the recovery period the
company uses for tax purposes:

 Light vehicles                           Service trucks, vans, sedans                         5
 Shop equipment                           Lifts, lathes, diagnostic scanners                   7
 Qualified improvement property           Leasehold build-outs, interior                       15
                                          renovations
 Building improvements                    Roofing, HVAC replacement,                           39
                                          structural work
 Section 197 intangibles                  Customer relationships, noncompete                   15
                                          agreements, trade names


Classification follows what the asset is and how the company uses it, not how the vendor invoices it. A
component that is a distinct asset with its own life — an HVAC unit installed in a building the company
owns — is capitalized in its own class; a repair that restores an existing asset is expensed.
A component of a larger asset is capitalized on its own when it has a distinct life or a distinct pattern of
use. A rooftop unit on a building the company owns is a building improvement in its own right; the
shelving installed in a cargo van is part of the van. When a project includes items of different classes, the
cost is allocated to the classes on the invoice schedule or, where the invoice does not separate them, on
the contractor's application for payment.
Repairs and maintenance that keep an asset in its ordinary condition are expensed in the period
performed. An expenditure that extends the life of an asset, increases its capacity, or adapts it to a new
use is capitalized and depreciated over its own life. Ordinary turnarounds of service equipment — brake
work, fluid services, tire replacement — are repairs, not improvements, regardless of their cost.

0.3.2 3.2 Listed Property Substantiation
Property the company operates that can serve personal as well as business purposes carries a
substantiation burden the company takes seriously. Tax positions for listed property must be based on
contemporaneous records of actual use, not on planning estimates or unadjusted carry-forward files.
The fleet office maintains the usage records for every vehicle: GPS telematics where the vehicle carries
a unit, and a manual mileage log where it does not. Drivers of vehicles without GPS units record
odometer readings at the start and end of each trip, and the branch office reconciles the logs to fuel card
purchases monthly. The fleet office summarizes each vehicle's business and personal miles for the year
after the year ends and files the summary with the vehicle's record.
Mileage records are retained for the period this manual requires in section
 1. A vehicle whose records for a year are incomplete is reported to the

Controller, who decides how the company will substantiate its use for that year before any return
position is taken.

Book lives are set by class at acquisition and reviewed for reasonableness when an asset's use changes:

The company claims cost recovery on its federal return under the methods the Internal Revenue Service

The depreciation and amortization workpapers are prepared by the Fixed Asset Accountant from the
fixed asset register and reviewed before the return goes out. The workpapers carry, for every asset,
vehicle, and amortization item: the description, the class, the cost, the date the item was placed in
service, the method and recovery period, the amount claimed for the current year, and the accumulated
recovery to the end of the year.

0.3.4 3.4 Disposals and Retirements
An asset is removed from the register when it is sold, traded, scrapped, or retired from service. The
retirement entry carries the date of disposal, the proceeds if any, and the accumulated recovery at
disposal; the gain or loss is computed in the module and posted to the general ledger. A vehicle
retirement is confirmed by the fleet office before the entry is made, and the vehicle's title, plate, and
telematics unit are cleared in the fleet register the same week.
A fully recovered asset that is still in service stays in the register with its cost and full accumulated
recovery until it is disposed of; it is not removed merely because its recovery period has ended.

0.3.5 3.5 Capital Projects
Construction projects, build-outs, and equipment installation projects over $50,000 are tracked in capital
project files while they run. Each project file carries the approval, the contracts, the applications for
payment, the change orders, and the closeout certificate. Costs sit in construction in progress until the
project is complete and the asset is placed in service, at which point the costs are classified to the fixed
asset register by class and recovery begins.

0.4 4 Fleet Asset Management Procedure
The fleet is the company's working plant. Crews, tools, and parts move on it, and the records in this
section are what let the company bill jobs accurately, insure the fleet correctly, and answer for its use
when a customer, an auditor, or the tax return asks.
The company operates a fleet of service trucks, vans, and sedans assigned to crews, service calls, and
staff. The fleet office, under the Fleet Office Coordinator, keeps the fleet records this section requires.

0.4.1 4.1 Vehicle Records and Registrations
The fleet office keeps a register of every unit in the fleet: the unit number, the plate number, the vehicle
identification number, the permanent fixed-asset tag, the body type, the dates of purchase and of release
to service, and the usage records section 4.4 describes. The register is reconciled to the titles and
registrations the company holds, and each vehicle's registration renewal is calendared sixty days ahead.
Titles are held by the Controller's office. Insurance certificates are kept with the vehicle file. A vehicle
acquired for the fleet is added to the register when it is purchased; its fixed asset tag is assigned at the
same time and does not change for the life of the vehicle.
A unit receives its fixed asset tag when it is added to the fleet register, from the same number series the
fixed asset module assigns. The tag is lettered on the vehicle's B-pillar and recorded on the title file, the
insurance schedule, and the telematics account. A vehicle that is replaced does not pass its tag to the
replacement; the replacement takes the next tag in the series, and the retired unit keeps its tag in the
retired records.
Vehicle records are matched to fixed assets by permanent unit asset identification number, not by plate
or registration number; plate numbers may be reassigned when a vehicle is replaced.
When a vehicle is replaced, the fleet office may transfer the plate of the retired unit to the replacement
for the convenience of yard parking, fuel cards, and gate access. The register carries the transfer in the
notes of both records: the retired unit's record states the plate was transferred and to which unit, and the
replacement's record states the plate was received and from which unit.
The fleet office reconciles the vehicle register to the fixed asset register twice a year. The reconciliation
lists every unit and every fixed asset vehicle record and shows which records match and which do not,
with the status of each vehicle as the two registers carry it.

0.4.2 4.3 Readiness and Service Dates
A vehicle is not in service the day it is invoiced. The fleet office records two dates for every unit: the
date of purchase or invoice, and the date the vehicle was registered, outfitted, and released to service.
Outfitting includes the installation of shelving, racks, crane bodies, livery, and telematics units the
vehicle needs for the work it is assigned.
The readiness date is recorded by the Fleet Office Coordinator when the vehicle is released to a branch,
and the release is confirmed by the branch manager who receives it. The fleet register carries both dates
for every unit; the purchase file carries the invoice and the outfitting work orders.
Outfitting work is authorized on a work order that names the unit, the upfitting vendor, and the scope.
When the work is done, the vendor invoice and the work order go to the fleet file, and the fleet office
records the release date in the register. A unit that arrives equipped from the dealer is released the week
its registration clears, on the same two-date rule.

0.4.3 4.4 Business Use and Mileage Logs
The fleet office collects usage for every unit for every month it is in service. Vehicles with GPS
telematics report business and personal miles by trip; vehicles without a unit run on the manual mileage
log the driver keeps in the vehicle. The fleet office summarizes each unit's business miles, personal
miles, and total miles for the calendar year and files the summary with the unit's record.
Company policy prohibits personal use of company vehicles except for de minimis commute use by the
assigned operator, and prohibits any personal use of the outfitted cargo vans. An operator whose
personal miles exceed the policy allowance is reported to the branch manager and the Controller.

0.5 5 Tax Filing and Review
0.5.1 5.1 Workpaper Review
The tax workpapers are reviewed before the return package goes to the outside CPA firm. The review is
performed by a person who did not prepare the workpapers. The reviewer works from the schedules and
the source documents, applies the requirements the return is filed under, and records what was checked
on the review checklist for the year.
A review that finds a position the workpapers do not support states the finding, the correction required,
and the dollar effect of the correction. The reviewer's findings go to the preparer for correction and, once
corrected, to the Controller with the return package. The return package is not released until every
finding is resolved or the Controller has accepted it in writing.

0.5.2 5.1.1 The filing calendar
The return calendar for each year is set at the January close and runs backwards from the filing deadline.
The internal review is calendared to leave the outside CPA firm two working weeks with the package. A
calendar that slips is reported to the Controller the day it slips, with the reason and the recovery date.

0.5.3 5.2 Sign-off Authority
The Corporate Controller signs the federal return package on behalf of the company and authorizes the
outside CPA firm to e-file. The Controller may delegate the preparation and assembly of the package but
not the signing authority. The outside CPA firm is engaged by letter and reviews the return package
before e-filing; the firm's review does not replace the company's own review under section 5.1.
Sign-offs are recorded with the signer's initials and the date. A signature on a schedule or a checklist line
means the signer performed the steps that line names. Delegations of signing authority are in writing,
name the matters delegated, and expire at the end of the fiscal year unless renewed.

0.6.1 Appendix A: Definitions
Fixed asset register. The accounting record of the property and equipment the company capitalizes,
carrying each item's description, class, cost, placed-in-service date, method, and accumulated recovery.
Fleet register. The fleet office's record of every vehicle the company operates, keyed by unit number
and carrying the plate, the vehicle identification number, the fixed-asset tag, and the usage records.
Workpapers. The schedules the finance department prepares to support the figures on the return.

0.6.2 Appendix B: Schedule Content Requirements
Every schedule named below carries its header fields (company, period, preparer, reviewer, date) and
the content the table states. A schedule that cannot be completed as specified is returned to the preparer
with the missing items noted, not completed by the reviewer.
Fixed asset detail. One row per asset, vehicle, or amortization item in the register.

Amortization schedule. One row per intangible per month of the year: the monthly amount, the

reviewer's initials, and the date.
Fleet usage summary. One row per unit per year: business miles, personal miles, total miles, the
business-use percentage, and the source of the measurement, filed with the vehicle's record.
Reconciliation of the registers. One row per unit and per fixed asset vehicle record, showing the match
or the reason the two registers carry the vehicle differently, prepared by the fleet office each half year.

0.6.3 Appendix C: Definitions of Records
Cost segregation study. An engineering study that allocates a building or build-out cost among the
classes of property it contains, used to classify costs for recovery purposes.
Capitalized cost. The invoice price plus the costs to acquire and ready an asset for its use, net of
discounts and trade-in allowances.
Construction in progress. The account that carries project costs while a project runs, before they are
classified to the fixed asset register.
Retired record. The fleet register entry of a unit that has been disposed of, kept for the retention period
with its plate and mileage history.
