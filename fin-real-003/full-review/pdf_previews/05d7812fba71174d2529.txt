 1 Purpose and Scope
 This manual states how the Environmental Health Division of the Pima County Health Department
 conducts environmental public health surveillance and administers the school outdoor activity
 surveillance program. It compiles the former Environmental Health Surveillance Standard Operating
 Procedure and the School Outdoor Activity Surveillance Codebook into one controlled document, so
 that division staff and school reporting contacts work from a single set of definitions and procedures.
 The manual applies to all division personnel who collect, review, analyse or report environmental health
 data, and to the school site staff who submit outdoor activity reports to the division. It covers the
 county's routine surveillance data sources, the annual program planning cycle, the review of
 operational records, and the standards that published analyses follow. It does not govern clinical care,
 individual patient management or enforcement actions, which are handled under separate division
 policies.
 Compliance with this manual is a condition of the division's data-sharing agreements with participating
 school districts and reporting facilities.

 2 Definitions
 The following definitions apply throughout this manual and in every report the division publishes.
  • Air Quality Index (AQI). The index the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency uses to
    communicate daily air quality, divided into six colour-coded categories that correspond to levels of
    health concern.

  • Daily AQI value. The AQI value reported for a county for a calendar day, together with the
    category, the defining parameter and the monitoring site that defined it.

  • Defining parameter. The pollutant whose sub-index set the day's AQI value, as recorded in the
    daily county data the division obtains from EPA AirData.

  • Syndromic surveillance. The ongoing collection of emergency-department visit records classified
    by presenting complaint, used to monitor community health conditions without waiting for definitive
    laboratory results.

  • Asthma-specific emergency-department visit. An emergency-department visit record classified
    by the syndromic surveillance registry under the asthma case definition in the registry's classifier
    version noted on each extract.

  • Outdoor activity. Any scheduled activity of a school site that takes place outdoors, including
    recess, physical education classes, athletic practices, scheduled athletic events and field trips.

  • School site. A reporting location within a school district that submits outdoor activity reports to the
    division under section 7.

  • County cancellation day. For county-level analyses, a cancellation day is a calendar date on
    which at least one school in the county cancelled outdoor activities; multiple school-site records for
    the same date are one county cancellation day.

 3 Roles and Responsibilities
 Division Director, Environmental Health. Approves the annual surveillance plan, signs off revisions
 of this manual and receives the operational record reviews prepared under section 6.
 Lead Biostatistician. Designs the division's analyses, prepares the analytic datasets used in published
 reports, documents the methods and assumptions behind each estimate, and answers for the statistical
 content of deliverables sent outside the division.
 Surveillance Analyst. Maintains the routine data extracts described in section 4, keeps the extract log
 current and prepares the data quality checks described in section 8.
 School Liaison. Maintains the roster of school site reporters, trains new reporting contacts and is the
 first point of contact for questions about the outdoor activity log.
 School site reporters. Submit outdoor activity reports as described in section 7, and tell the School
 Liaison when a reporting contact changes.

 4 Routine Environmental Health Surveillance Data Sources
 The division maintains six routine data sources for environmental health surveillance. Each source's
 refresh cadence and owning role are recorded in the extract log kept by the Surveillance Analyst.
  • EPA AirData daily AQI by county. Daily county-level AQI records obtained from the U.S.
    Environmental Protection Agency's AirData system. The division's working copy of this file is
    prepared by the analytics team and carries the fields the EPA publishes: state and county names,
    state and county codes, date, AQI value, category, defining parameter, defining site and the number
    of sites reporting.

  • EPA AirData annual AQI by county. The annual county summary published by the EPA, with the
    days in each AQI category, the maximum, median and ninetieth percentile AQI values and the
    count of days by defining parameter.

  • Census county reference files. The county FIPS reference and the county population estimates
    published by the U.S. Census Bureau, used to identify counties and to describe the resident
    population the division serves.

  • AirNow AQI technical assistance document. The EPA's technical assistance document for the
    reporting of daily air quality, which defines the AQI categories, their index value ranges and the
    colour and health message that goes with each category.

  • The department's syndromic surveillance registry. Emergency-department visit records from
    reporting facilities, aggregated by the registry into daily counts by county, with the reporting facility
    completeness figures described in section 8.

  • The school outdoor activity log. The reports school sites submit under section 7, one record per
    school site and date.

 When a source publishes a correction or a revised edition, the Surveillance Analyst replaces the
 division's copy, records the change in the extract log and tells the Lead Biostatistician which pending
 analyses are affected.

 5 Program Planning Assumptions
 The division's planning documents set the operating parameters the program is budgeted and staffed
 around: the surveillance seasons, the reporting schedule, the outreach calendar and the exposure
 classifications used in program planning. A planning assumption is a statement of how the program
 expects to operate, written before the year's records exist, and it is kept in the planning document that
 adopted it.
 Planning assumptions are working positions, not findings. Planning assumptions used for exposure
 classification are checked against observed operational records; when they disagree, the analysis
 reports both the planned threshold and the threshold estimated from observed operations.
 The biannual program plan records each assumption still in force, the document that adopted it and the
 year it was written, so that a reader can tell an assumed parameter from a measured one.

 6 Operational Record Review
 The Lead Biostatistician conducts operational record reviews on a twice-yearly schedule. Reviews
 cover the most recent complete calendar year.
 A review states, for each assumption examined: the source and date of the planning assumption, the
 records examined, what those records show, and whether the assumption held. Reviews are filed with
 the division's planning materials and summarised for the Division Director.
 Where an operational record and a planning assumption disagree, the disagreement is reported to the
 Division Director in writing before the planning meeting at which the assumption may be revised, so that
 the meeting decides with both positions in front of it.

 7 School Outdoor Activity Surveillance Procedures
 Participating school sites report to the division on the days the division requests a report. A report
 names the school site, the date, whether outdoor activity was cancelled, the reported reason and the
 activities affected, and the person or role who filed it. Sites file one record for their site and date; a
 district with several participating sites receives one request per site.
 The log covers reported outdoor activities year-round, including summer sessions and Saturday
 athletics, so a report can be requested for any day on which a site has outdoor activities scheduled.
 Days on which a site has nothing scheduled outdoors generate no record.
 The School Liaison sends the season's reporting schedule to site reporters before each school term
 and again before summer sessions. Reporters file by the end of the reporting day; late reports are
 added to the log with the date they cover, not the date they arrived.
 The division treats the log as the operational record of the school outdoor activity program. Published
 summaries of the log follow section 9.

 8 Data Quality and Completeness
 The registry records, for every county and day, the number of reporting facilities that submitted records
 and the number expected to report. The completeness share is the reports received divided by the
 reports expected.
 The Surveillance Analyst reviews facility completeness each month. Facilities that miss a reporting
 cycle are contacted by the School Liaison's counterpart in syndromic surveillance; facilities that miss
 two consecutive cycles are escalated to the division's data-sharing coordinator.
 An extract's version identifier names the registry classifier version it was produced under. The analyst
 records any change of classifier version in the extract log, because a change in the case definition can
 move a count from one syndrome class to another.

 9 Analysis and Reporting Standards
 Every analysis the division publishes states the data sources it used, the calendar period it covers and
 the definitions of every measure it reports. An analysis that merges sources keeps the merged dataset,
 so a reviewer can retrace the work.
 A published rate names its numerator, its denominator and the period it covers, and states whether the
 denominator is a population, a count of days or a count of records. A published count states the records
 it was taken over.
 Annual Air Quality Index summary values for a county evaluation are drawn from the single EPA
 Annual Summary row for the county named in the evaluation and for the calendar year the evaluation
 examines.
 Model-based estimates are reported with their uncertainty. An analysis that fits a statistical model
 states the model form, the outcome and exposure definitions, the estimation sample and the
 diagnostics the analyst ran, in enough detail for a qualified reader to judge the fit.
 Reports carry the division's document control block: title, author, date, document identifier and revision,
 so that later readers can tell which edition of an analysis they hold.

 10 Privacy and Confidentiality
 The surveillance registry holds health information protected by federal and state law. Analyses are
 conducted on aggregates only; a published table or figure does not identify a person, a household or an
 individual facility's patients.
 Small cell counts are suppressed in any output released outside the division. Requests for surveillance
 data from outside the department are referred to the division's privacy officer and answered under the
 department's public records procedure.
 Staff with registry access complete privacy training under section 12 before access is granted, and
 access is reviewed annually.

 11 Records Retention
 Routine surveillance extracts, the extract log and the school outdoor activity log are retained for seven
 years from the end of the calendar year they cover, then destroyed under the department's records
 schedule.
 Published reports, operational record reviews and the planning materials of the biannual program plan
 are retained permanently. Analytic datasets behind a published report are retained with the report they
 support.
 Destruction of records is logged by the destroying staff member and countersigned by the division's
 records coordinator.

 12 Training
 New division staff read this manual and complete the department's data governance training before
 working with surveillance data. School site reporters receive the reporting guide and a short briefing
 from the School Liaison before their first reporting season.
 The division runs an annual refresher covering changes to this manual, the current reporting schedule
 and the data quality procedures of section 8. Attendance is recorded in the department's training
 system.
 A school site that repeatedly files incomplete reports receives a follow-up visit from the School Liaison
 before the next reporting season.
